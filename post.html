Post a paper so LingFeed finds it

LingFeed doesn’t host papers. It lists new papers from a few repositories. Post your paper in one of them, in the right place, and it appears on LingFeed by itself, usually within an hour. You don’t need to tell us.

This page explains how to post to Zenodo, PsyArXiv, SocArXiv and arXiv so that LingFeed sees your paper. (Papers posted on LingBuzz are picked up automatically, with nothing special to do.)

Which one should I choose?

  • Zenodo is open to any kind of work, takes the least time to set up, and gives your paper a DOI. For LingFeed to see it, it must be accepted into Zenodo’s Linguistics community (see below).
  • PsyArXiv suits work with a psychological side (processing, acquisition, experimental work). SocArXiv suits work with a social-science side (sociolinguistics, language policy, language and society). Both are run on the Open Science Framework (OSF), both check papers before posting them, and both give a DOI.
  • arXiv suits computational work. It has the strictest rules, and it is the least visible source on LingFeed: arXiv papers are hidden unless the reader ticks the arXiv box, because arXiv adds about 200 papers a day in its language category.

You can post the same paper in more than one place. LingFeed shows it once, with a badge for each (see Tips for every repository).

Zenodo

What LingFeed looks for: records in the Zenodo community Linguistics. A paper posted on Zenodo but not in this community is not listed.

  1. Sign up or log in at zenodo.org. You can log in with an ORCID or GitHub account.
  2. Go to the Linguistics community and click New upload. Starting from the community page is what links your paper to it; the community’s name then appears at the top of the upload form.
  3. Add your PDF and fill in the form. The fields that matter most for LingFeed are the title, the authors, the description (your abstract) and the keywords: LingFeed shows them and searches them. For Resource type, choose Publication → Preprint for a preprint, or whatever fits (journal article, book chapter, thesis…).
  4. Click Submit for review, tick the boxes, and confirm. The community’s curators then review the submission. Your paper is published on Zenodo when they accept it, and it appears on LingFeed within about 15 minutes after that.

Already posted on Zenodo without the community? Open your record, click the cog-wheel icon in its Communities box, choose Submit to community, and pick Linguistics. Once accepted, it shows up on LingFeed at the date you first posted it, so it may appear further down the list rather than at the top.

Posting a revision? On your record, click New version, rather than starting a new upload. That way LingFeed lists it as an update of the same paper (“Recently updated”), and readers find all versions in one place. If you start a fresh upload instead, LingFeed treats it as a different paper.

LingFeed also lists the Language Science Press community, but that one holds the press’s own books; authors don’t submit there directly.

PsyArXiv and SocArXiv

What LingFeed looks for: preprints whose subject (“discipline”) is Linguistics, or any subfield listed under it. A paper without that subject is not listed, even if it is about language.

  1. Make an account at osf.io (both servers use the same account). SocArXiv requires the submitting author to have a public ORCID linked to their OSF profile, so connect your ORCID in your OSF profile settings first.
  2. Go to PsyArXiv or SocArXiv and click Add a preprint.
  3. Fill in the title, abstract, authors and tags (keywords). When you reach Discipline (subjects), type Linguistics in the search box and select it. You can add narrower subjects under Linguistics too; any of them counts.
  4. Upload your PDF, choose a licence, and submit.
  5. A moderator checks the paper before it becomes public; this usually takes from a day to a few days. Once it is approved, it appears on LingFeed within about an hour.

Posting a revision? Open your preprint and use Create new version (it also has to be approved). LingFeed lists it as an update of the same paper.

Which of the two? SocArXiv now accepts only the social sciences, education and law, and may turn down papers that are mainly humanities (for example philology or literary topics). PsyArXiv expects a connection to psychology. If your paper fits neither well, Zenodo is the easier choice.

arXiv

What LingFeed looks for: papers in arXiv’s category cs.CL (Computation and Language), either as the main category or as a cross-list. LingFeed keeps only about the last week of arXiv papers, and they are shown only when readers tick the arXiv box.

  1. Register at arxiv.org.
  2. Start a new submission and choose Computer Science → Computation and Language (cs.CL) as the primary category. If your paper belongs mainly in another category, you can add cs.CL as a cross-list instead.
  3. Endorsement. First-time submitters to a category need an endorsement from someone who already publishes there. arXiv will email you a link to forward to a possible endorser — ideally someone you know who works in your area. Allow time for this.
  4. Upload your paper (arXiv prefers LaTeX source, but accepts PDF), fill in the title, authors and abstract, and submit. arXiv announces new papers Sunday to Thursday evenings (US Eastern time); LingFeed picks them up within about an hour of that.

Rules to know. arXiv has tightened its rules recently. The full paper must be available in English. Since October 2026, each person can submit at most two papers a month. In computer science, which includes cs.CL, surveys and position papers are accepted only once they have passed peer review at a journal or conference. Check arXiv’s help pages for the current details.

Posting a revision? Use Replace on your arXiv user page. Version 2 and later count as an update on LingFeed.

Tips for every repository

  • Use the same title everywhere. If you post a paper in two places (say LingBuzz and Zenodo), LingFeed joins the copies into one listing when the titles match (capitals, accents and punctuation don’t matter) and the authors share a name. A changed title means two listings.
  • Write a real abstract and keywords. LingFeed searches titles, keywords and abstracts, so readers find your paper through them.
  • Spell your name the same way each time, so an author search finds all your papers.
  • Post revisions as new versions of the same record, not as new uploads. LingFeed then shows the paper as updated and moves it back near the top of the “New and Updated” view.

My paper hasn’t appeared

  • Zenodo: has the Linguistics community accepted it? Open your record: the community is listed on the right if it has. Pending submissions are not public yet.
  • PsyArXiv/SocArXiv: has a moderator approved it, and is Linguistics among its subjects? You can add a subject by editing the preprint.
  • arXiv: is the arXiv box ticked on LingFeed? Is cs.CL one of its categories? Has arXiv announced it yet?
  • Papers are listed by when they were posted, not when LingFeed found them, so an older paper may sit further down. Try searching for its title.
