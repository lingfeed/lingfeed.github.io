Post a paper so LingFeed finds it

LingFeed doesn’t host papers. It shows papers from lingbuzz, Zenodo, PsyArXiv, SocArXiv and arXiv, usually within an hour of posting.

This page explains how to post to Zenodo, PsyArXiv, SocArXiv and arXiv so that LingFeed sees your paper. (Papers posted on LingBuzz are picked up automatically.)

Choosing a repository

As we discuss on our Why LingFeed? page, linguists have a number of options for modern, stable repositories that host linguistics work.

  • Zenodo is open to any kind of research, takes the least time to set up, and gives your paper a DOI. For LingFeed to see it, submit it to LingFeed’s own Zenodo community (see below) or to the community called “Linguistics”.
  • PsyArXiv is aimed at “psychological research”; see its policies here. SocArXiv “accepts scholarly work in the social sciences, broadly defined”; see its moderation policy here. Both are run on the Open Science Framework (OSF), both have moderators check papers before posting them, and both give a DOI. Both also have explicit policies about the use of AI/LLMs, which you can find at the links above.
  • arXiv (Language and Computation) may be appropriate for computational work. It is the least visible source on LingFeed: arXiv papers are hidden unless the reader ticks the arXiv box, because arXiv adds about 200 papers a day in its language category.

You can post the same paper in more than one place. LingFeed shows one entry for the paper, with a badge for each source; readers can click through to the source they prefer. (See Tips for every repository.)

Zenodo

What LingFeed looks for: records in a Zenodo community that we follow. We recommend the community LingFeed, which we run for exactly this purpose; papers in Zenodo’s Linguistics community are listed too. A paper posted on Zenodo is only listed if it's in one of these communities.

  1. Sign up or log in at zenodo.org. You can log in with an ORCID or GitHub account.
  2. Go to the LingFeed community and click New upload. Starting from the community page is what links your paper to it; the community’s name then appears at the top of the upload form.
  3. Under "Files", add your PDF. The rest of the upload form is long, but most of it can be set aside. You have to specify:
    • whether or not you already have a DOI (it will make you one if you don't already have one)
    • what sort of thing your upload is (for preprints, pick Publication/Preprint)
    • the title
    • the authors
    For LingFeed visibility, we strongly recommend that you also include the description (your abstract) and the keywords, because both of these are things that LingFeed shows and searches. Keywords go in the "Keywords and subjects" field. Enter keywords one at a time, typing return after each entry.
  4. Click Submit for review (all the way at the bottom), tick the boxes, and confirm. The LingFeed team then gets a request to add it to the community. Your paper is published on Zenodo when we accept it, and it appears on LingFeed within about 15 minutes after that.

Already posted on Zenodo without the community? Open your record, click the cog-wheel icon in its Communities box, choose Submit to community, and pick LingFeed. Once accepted, it shows up on LingFeed at the date you first posted it, so it may appear further down the list rather than at the top. (A paper can be cross-listed in several Zenodo communities but you have to add them one at a time.)

Make it easier next time! We can add you as a member of the LingFeed community on Zenodo, which will allow you to post freely without the need for moderator approval. If you are a linguist, please join us! Make a Zenodo account and then email us at lingfeedteam@gmail.com to request to be added. (Surprisingly, there is no way to request to be added within Zenodo itself. You gotta email.)

Posting a revision? On your record, click New version, rather than starting a new upload. That way LingFeed lists it as an update of the same paper (“Recently updated”), and readers find all versions in one place. If you start a fresh upload instead, LingFeed treats it as a different paper.

LingFeed also lists the Language Science Press community, but authors don’t submit there directly.

PsyArXiv and SocArXiv

What LingFeed looks for: preprints whose subject (“discipline”) is Linguistics, or any subfield listed under it. A paper without that subject is not listed, even if it is about linguistics.

  1. Make an account at osf.io (both servers use the same account). SocArXiv requires the submitting author to have a public ORCID linked to their OSF profile, so connect your ORCID in your OSF profile settings first.
  2. Go to PsyArXiv or SocArXiv and click Add a preprint.
  3. Fill in the title, abstract, authors and tags (keywords). When you reach Discipline (subjects), type Linguistics in the search box and select it. You can add narrower subjects under Linguistics too; any of them counts.
  4. Upload your PDF, choose a licence, and submit.
  5. A moderator checks the paper before it becomes public; this usually takes from a day to a few days. Once it is approved, it appears on LingFeed within about an hour.

Posting a revision? Open your preprint and use Create new version (it also has to be approved). LingFeed lists it as an update of the same paper.

Which of the two? PsyArXiv expects a connection to psychology. SocArXiv accepts only the social sciences, education and law, and may turn down papers that are have a more humanities vibe in the moderator's opinion. If your linguistics paper isn't either of those things, Zenodo is the easier choice.

arXiv

What LingFeed looks for: papers in arXiv’s category cs.CL (Computation and Language), either as the main category or as a cross-list. LingFeed keeps only about the last week of arXiv papers, and they are shown only when readers tick the arXiv box.

  1. Register at arxiv.org.
  2. Start a new submission and choose Computer Science → Computation and Language (cs.CL) as the primary category. If your paper belongs mainly in another category, you can add cs.CL as a cross-list instead.
  3. Endorsement. First-time submitters to a category need an endorsement from someone who already publishes there. arXiv will email you a link to forward to a possible endorser — ideally someone you know who works in your research area.
  4. Upload your paper (arXiv prefers LaTeX source, but accepts PDF), fill in the title, authors and abstract, and submit. arXiv announces new papers Sunday to Thursday evenings (US Eastern time); LingFeed picks them up within about an hour of that.

Rules to know. arXiv has tightened its rules recently. The full paper must be available in English. Since October 2026, each person can submit at most two papers a month. In computer science, which includes cs.CL, surveys and position papers are accepted only once they have passed peer review at a journal or conference. Check arXiv’s help pages for the current details.

Posting a revision? Use Replace on your arXiv user page. Version 2 and later count as an update on LingFeed.

Tips for every repository

  • Use the same title everywhere. If you post a paper in two places (say LingBuzz and Zenodo), LingFeed joins the copies into one listing when the titles match (capitals, accents and punctuation don’t matter) and the authors share a name. A change to the title will trigger a new listing, not an "updated" flag on the previous listing.
  • Don't omit an abstract or keywords. LingFeed searches titles, keywords and abstracts, so readers find your paper through them.
  • Spell your name the same way each time, so an author search finds all your papers.
  • Post revisions as new versions of the same record, not as new uploads. LingFeed then shows the paper as updated and moves it back near the top of the “New and Updated” view.

My paper hasn’t appeared

  • Zenodo: has the LingFeed or Linguistics Zenodo community accepted it? Open your record: the community is listed on the right if it has. Pending submissions are not public yet.
  • PsyArXiv/SocArXiv: has a moderator approved it, and is Linguistics among its subjects? You can add a subject by editing the preprint.
  • arXiv: is the arXiv box ticked on LingFeed? Is cs.CL one of its categories? Has arXiv announced it yet?
  • Papers are listed by when they were posted, not when LingFeed found them, so an older paper may sit further down. Try searching for its title.
