LingFeed exists to make linguistics preprints findable wherever they are hosted.

Choose the repository that's best for you; we'll help linguists find your work

Why preprints

The sharing of as-yet unpublished work has played a central role in theoretical linguistics for decades, as it also has in fields such as physics, mathematics, and computer science. Modern pre-print repositories provide a convenient way to share drafts of unpublished work, along with data sets and other resources.

Which repository should I put my preprint in?

Linguists have a choice of repositories for their preprints. Perhaps the best-known preprint repository among linguists is LingBuzz. However, there are also significant numbers of linguistics preprints in other places, including Zenodo, psyArXiv, socArXiv, and arXiv.

Some basic factors you might consider in choosing a repository are whether the work will be assigned a persistent identifier, such as a DOI, so that links to a record are not lost over time; whether the preprint server itself is reliably online and working; and whether files uploaded in error can be deleted.

All of the repositories listed above meet these requirements, except LingBuzz. Indeed there are quite a few ways that LingBuzz falls behind the competition as a repository:

LingFeed is a tool for preprint discovery

Despite these well-known, long-standing issues with LingBuzz, it is still very popular with linguists. Why? Because it works well as a discovery tool: posting your paper to LingBuzz raises the chances that a large number of linguists will see it.

This is where LingFeed comes in. LingFeed exists to help move the linguistics community toward a world where preprints posted on non-LingBuzz repositories are equally prominent and findable. You might want to post your paper on a repository other than LingBuzz for various reasons, including:

  • The factors listed above are important to you (e.g. long-term viability, assignment of a DOI)
  • Your research funder requires the use of a specific repository
  • LingBuzz just does not work: it returns mysterious error messages when you attempt to post your paper, or refuses to correctly recognize your name (meaning you cannot be listed as an author), etc etc

None of these factors make someone's work less relevant to the community of linguists. As a community, we need a tool to help us find preprints both on and off of LingBuzz. LingFeed aims to be that tool.

You are needed

For LingFeed to succeed, it needs to become the place where linguists look to see what is new in the field. Do you check LingBuzz on a regular basis? Check LingFeed instead. You will still see everything posted to LingBuzz, AND papers in other places. You can still search in the ways you would search on LingBuzz, AND using wildcards (*) and booleans (AND/OR).

If LingBuzz is down, you will still see the history of listings LingFeed has captured, though you will not be able to access the papers themselves. LingFeed is not a repository and does not host papers.
